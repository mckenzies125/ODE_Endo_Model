# Modeling Estradiol and Endometriosis Lesion Dynamics

This project develops a mathematical model of the cyclic relationship between **estradiol (E2)** and **endometriosis lesion growth**.

Building on published models of the menstrual cycle and endometrial growth, we extended the existing mathematical framework to incorporate endometriosis lesion growth and lesion-associated estradiol production. The resulting system creates a feedback loop in which lesions contribute additional estradiol while estradiol promotes continued tissue growth.

The model was implemented in **MATLAB and Simulink** and used to investigate lesion growth, endometrial growth, long-term hormonal dynamics, and the effects of periodic estradiol suppression.

## Research Question

Endometriosis is an estrogen-dependent condition in which endometrial-like tissue grows outside the uterus. Endometriosis lesions can contribute to local estrogen production, while increased estrogen exposure can promote continued tissue growth.

This creates a potential feedback relationship:

```text
       Estradiol (E2)
             ↓
       Lesion Growth
             ↓
       Lesion Volume
             ↓
Additional E2 Production
             ↺
```

This project asks:

**Can existing mathematical models of the menstrual cycle and endometrial growth be extended to represent the cyclic relationship between estradiol and endometriosis lesion growth?**

## Model Development

The project integrates and extends two previously published mathematical models.

### Menstrual Cycle Model

Margolskee and Selgrade (2013) developed a mathematical model of the female reproductive cycle that simulates fluctuations in reproductive hormones, including **estradiol (E2)** and **progesterone (P4)**.

These hormonal dynamics provide inputs for the tissue-growth components of our model.

### Endometrial Growth Model

Arbeláez-Gómez et al. (2022) developed a phenomenological model of endometrial growth and shedding during the menstrual cycle.

Their model uses estradiol and progesterone generated by the menstrual-cycle model to regulate endometrial tissue dynamics.

### Endometriosis Extension

We extended this framework by introducing equations representing:

- Proliferative lesion-cell growth
- Secretory lesion-cell growth
- Cellular products secreted by lesions
- Total endometriosis lesion volume

The model then feeds total lesion volume back into the estradiol equation, allowing lesion growth to contribute additional estradiol to the system.

## Mathematical Framework

Proliferative lesion-cell growth is modeled as:

\[
\frac{dM_{\chi proL}}{dt}
=
\mu_{proL}M_{\chi proL}
-
\mu_{secL}M_{\chi proL}
\]

Secretory lesion-cell growth is modeled as:

\[
\frac{dM_{\chi secL}}{dt}
=
Y_{\chi sec,\chi proL}\mu_{secL}M_{\chi proL}
-
\dot{m}_{6L}w_{\chi sec,6L}
\]

Cellular-product dynamics are modeled as:

\[
\frac{dM_{\chi pL}}{dt}
=
q_{pL}M_{\chi secL}
-
\dot{m}_{6L}w_{p,6L}
\]

Total lesion volume is calculated as:

\[
V_{TL}
=
\frac{M_{\chi proL}}{\rho_{\chi proL}}
+
\frac{M_{\chi secL}}{\rho_{\chi secL}}
+
\frac{M_{\chi pL}}{\rho_{\chi pL}}
\]

The original estradiol equation was then extended to incorporate lesion-associated estradiol production:

\[
E2 =
e_0 + e_1GrF + e_2DomF + e_3Lut_4 + e_4V_{TL} + e_5
\]

where:

- \(V_{TL}\) = total endometriosis lesion volume
- \(e_4\) = rate of estradiol secretion associated with lesions
- \(e_5\) = exogenous estradiol suppression term

The \(e_4V_{TL}\) term creates the central feedback mechanism of the proposed model.

## Implementation

The modeling workflow was implemented using **MATLAB and Simulink**.

We initially reproduced the menstrual-cycle model in MATLAB and attempted to reproduce the endometrial-growth model using the same approach. However, the combination of delay differential equations and stiff differential equations resulted in a runtime of approximately **9 hours to simulate only 10 days** of the menstrual cycle.

After approximately two weeks of troubleshooting, we contacted an author of the original endometrial-growth model and obtained its Simulink implementation. We then adapted that framework to incorporate:

- Endometriosis lesion dynamics
- Lesion-associated estradiol production
- Feedback between lesion volume and estradiol
- Exogenous estradiol suppression

## Simulation Experiments

The completed system was simulated across multiple time horizons to investigate:

- Endometriosis lesion volume
- Endometrial volume
- Estradiol levels
- Progesterone levels
- Long-term E2-to-P4 dynamics
- Response to periodic estradiol suppression

Lesion and hormonal dynamics were examined over simulations extending from **100 days to 10,000 days**.

## Results

### Lesion Growth

The model produced persistent endometriosis lesion growth over time.

Because lesion volume contributes additional estradiol to the system, the model establishes a feedback mechanism between estradiol production and lesion growth.

### Endometrial Growth

Endometrial volume also increased over time as lesion-associated estradiol was continuously introduced into the existing endometrial-growth model.

Together, these simulations reproduced the expected qualitative relationship in which increased estradiol exposure supports continued tissue growth.

### Long-Term Hormonal Dynamics

Long-term simulations produced gradual decreases in both estradiol and progesterone.

The decrease in estradiol was **unexpected**. Because endometriosis lesions were incorporated as an additional source of estradiol, increased rather than decreased E2 levels had initially been anticipated.

However, progesterone decreased more rapidly than estradiol, resulting in an increased **E2-to-P4 ratio** in the modeled endometriosis system.

The unexpected long-term estradiol behavior highlights an area where the model requires further investigation and refinement.

## Estradiol Suppression Experiment

The model was also used to simulate periodic exogenous estradiol suppression.

The suppression parameter was set to:

- \(e_5 = -12.5\) during days **1000–1030**
- \(e_5 = -10\) during days **2000–2030**
- \(e_5 = -10\) during days **3000–3030**

During each simulated suppression period, estradiol levels decreased.

Corresponding decreases were also observed in:

- **Endometriosis lesion volume**
- **Endometrial volume**

After suppression ended, estradiol levels increased as the simulated system returned toward its previous dynamics.

These experiments demonstrate how the model can be used to explore the behavior of lesion and endometrial growth under changes in estradiol exposure.

## Key Findings

The proposed model successfully created a mathematical feedback relationship between estradiol and endometriosis lesion growth.

The simulations demonstrated:

- Persistent lesion growth over time
- Increasing endometrial volume in the presence of lesions
- Feedback between lesion volume and estradiol production
- A higher long-term E2-to-P4 ratio
- Decreased estradiol during simulated periods of E2 suppression
- Decreased lesion and endometrial volume during E2 suppression
- Unexpected long-term estradiol behavior requiring further investigation

## Limitations

### Parameter Estimation

A major limitation was the lack of biologically established parameter values for the proposed lesion equations.

After searching the available literature, biologically validated parameters for lesion growth could not be identified. Parameters from the existing endometrial-growth model were therefore **scaled down by a factor of 10** for the proposed lesion model.

The resulting simulations should therefore be interpreted primarily as an exploration of the **qualitative behavior of the mathematical system**, rather than quantitatively validated predictions of disease progression.

### Data Availability

The project was also limited by the lack of publicly available endometriosis datasets.

A secondary predictive model using logistic regression was initially proposed, but this analysis could not be completed because suitable public data could not be identified.

### Unexpected Estradiol Dynamics

Although lesions were modeled as an additional source of estradiol, long-term E2 levels decreased rather than increased.

Understanding this behavior requires further investigation of the model structure, feedback mechanisms, and parameter assumptions.

## Future Work

Future extensions of this project could include:

- Estimating model parameters using biological or clinical data
- Performing formal parameter sensitivity analysis
- Investigating the unexpected long-term estradiol dynamics
- Validating simulated lesion growth against longitudinal patient data
- Modeling additional endometriosis treatments
- Representing different lesion types and disease stages
- Modeling the movement of endometrial tissue to different anatomical locations
- Refining the representation of local estradiol production

Greater availability of longitudinal endometriosis data would allow the model to move from qualitative simulation toward empirical parameter estimation and validation.
